# Final word on fast computers

In my opinion, if you're a software engineer, invest in a fast computer.

Big tech is slowing us down with sloppy code, and there's nothing you can do to stop the titanic from sinking.

Also, my philosophy is, you can do a lot more powerful things and learn really fast. You just don't realize it because you've never had enough power to try.

#### Some examples:

- CGI / Video editing
  Takes a lot of power, GPU, CPU, RAM. Extremely tedious and limiting without the power

- Local LLMs
  Extremely useful learning experience to tinker with these, even if you don't actually use them, just mess with them.

- CAD / Simulation software
  Even if you're not a mechanical engineer, you can still benefit from being able to run all the amazing simulation programs available for free.

- Big datasets
  Thousands of images, gigabytes of records, machine learning models. I can process these without sacrificing a whole day of computer usage. It's just running in the background, no problem.

Basically, once you have the power to do so, you'll get creative ideas in no time.

And lastly, you'll never know how many ideas you missed because you got burnt out waiting for things to load or compile.

Think about it: If you have a laptop, compared to my computer, I am having magnitudes less friction when I iterate my software.
Even slow software, to me, is fast. I will always have an advantage over you since I can be less fustrated and more engaged overall.

It's a slight boost, but it's a serious thing I encourage you to consider in your own setup if you can afford the expense!

And no, I don't really play videogames on it that much, but yes, it can run everything on max. I occasionally get hooked on a gaming spree, but it's been a while.
